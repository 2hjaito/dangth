## Part 2. Counting

### Question 1 
A string of 6 characters: 2 letters followed by 4 digits. How many different strings can be written if:

#### a. Letters and digits cannot repeat?

- Number of ways to choose and arrange 2 letters (no repeats):  
  $$A_2^{26} = 26 \times 25 = 650$$

- Number of ways to choose and arrange 4 digits (no repeats):  
  $$A_4^{10} = 10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7 = 5040$$

<!-- - Số cách chọn vị trí cho 2 chữ cái trong 6 vị trí:  
  $$\binom{6}{2} = 15$$ -->

👉 The total number of possible strings is:  
$$
A_2^{26} \times A_4^{10} \times = 650 \times 5040 = 3,\!276,\!000 
$$


> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  3,276,000
>

#### b. Letters and digits may repeat?

- Positions 1 and 2 in the string: each has 26 choices  
- Positions 3, 4, 5, and 6: each has 10 choices  

So the number of ways is:  
$$
26^2 \times 10^4 = 676 \times 10,\!000 = 6,\!760,\!000 \text{ ways}
$$

> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  6,760,000
>
---

#### c. Repeats allowed, but letters are limited to the 5 vowels (A, E, I, O, U) and digits

- Positions 1 and 2 in the string: each has 5 choices  
- Positions 3, 4, 5, and 6: each has 6 choices  

So the number of ways is:  
$$
5^2 \times 6^4 = 25 \times 1296 = 32,\!400 \text{ ways}
$$

> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  32,400
>

### Question 2 

How many binary strings of length 10 have the first and last bit equal to 1?



---

**Solution:**

- The first and last positions: each has 1 choice (both must be bit `1`)  
- The remaining 8 middle positions: each has 2 choices (`0` or `1`)

$$
\text{So the number of strings is: } 1 \times 2^8 = 256 \text{ strings}
$$


> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  256 strings
>

### Question 3

How many binary strings of length 10 **either** start with 3 zero bits **or** end with 2 zero bits?

---

**Solution:**

- $$A$$: The set of length-10 binary strings **starting with 3 zero bits** (`000xxxxxxx`)
- $$B$$: The set of length-10 binary strings **ending with 2 zero bits** (`xxxxxxxx00`)

Notation:

- $$A \cup B$$: The set of strings **in A or B or both** (logical "or" union)
- $$A \cap B$$: The set of strings **in both A and B** (logical "and" intersection)

---


**Computing the size of each set:**

- Total number of length-10 binary strings:  
  $$
  2^{10} = 1024
  $$

- Number of strings starting with `000`:  
  (First 3 bits fixed, 7 bits free)
  $$
  |A| = 2^7 = 128
  $$

- Number of strings ending with `00`:  
  (Last 2 bits fixed, 8 bits free)
  $$
  |B| = 2^8 = 256
  $$

- Number of strings both starting with `000` and ending with `00`:  
  (5 bits fixed, 5 bits in the middle free)
  $$
  |A \cap B| = 2^5 = 32
  $$

---

**Applying the inclusion-exclusion formula:**

Formula:
$$
|A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B|
$$

Substituting the numbers:
$$
|A \cup B| = 2^7 + 2^8 - 2^5 = 128 + 256 - 32 = 352
$$


---

> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  There are **352 binary strings of length 10** that **either start with 3 zero bits or end with 2 zero bits**.
>

### Question 4  
How many distinct character strings can be obtained by rearranging the letters of the word **SUCCESS**?

---

**Solution:**

- We have:  
  - 3 occurrences of **S**  
  - 2 occurrences of **C**  
  - 1 occurrence of **U**  
  - 1 occurrence of **E**

- So the number of arrangements is:

$$
\frac{7!}{3! \cdot 2! \cdot 1! \cdot 1!} = \frac{5040}{6 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1} = \frac{5040}{12} = 420 \text{ ways}
$$



> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  There are **420** ways.
>
---

### Question 5:
How many different arrangements are there for the letters in the word **VISITTING**?

---

 **Solution:**

- We have:
  - 3 occurrences of **I**
  - 2 occurrences of **T**
  - 1 occurrence of **V**
  - 1 occurrence of **S**
  - 1 occurrence of **N**
  - 1 occurrence of **G**

- So the number of arrangements is:

$$
\frac{9!}{3! \cdot 2! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1!} = \frac{362880}{12} = 30240 \text{ ways}
$$


> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  30240 ways
>

---

#### **Bonus question:**  
How many arrangements are there such that **the 3 letters I always stay next to each other**?

---

- We treat the 3 letters **I** as **a single block** (call it "super I") => [**III**] 
- We're then left with:
  - 2 occurrences of **T**
  - 1 occurrence of **V**
  - 1 occurrence of **S**
  - 1 occurrence of **N**
  - 1 occurrence of **G**
  - 1 **I** block

→ Giving **7 elements** to arrange in total

- So the number of arrangements is:

$$
\frac{7!}{2! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1!} = \frac{5040}{2} = 2520 \text{ ways}
$$


> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  2520 ways
>

### Question 6
Same exercise as above, applied to the word **MASSASAUGA** and the letter **A**.

---

**Solution:**

- We treat the **4 occurrences of A** as **a single block** (one unit)
- We're then left with:
  - 3 occurrences of **S**
  - 1 occurrence of **M**
  - 1 occurrence of **U**
  - 1 occurrence of **G**
  - 1 **A** block

→ Giving **7 elements** to arrange in total

- So the number of arrangements is:

$$
\frac{7!}{3! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1! \cdot 1!} = \frac{5040}{6} = 840 \text{ ways}
$$

> [!note] **Answer:**
>
>  840 ways
>

## Chapter 3: Logical Reasoning

### Question 3  

**Premises:**

1. If it is **not raining** or there is **no fog**, then the **boat race** will be held and the **rescue demonstration** will also proceed  
2. If the boat race is held, then **the prize will be awarded**  
3. **The prize was not awarded**


Use rules of inference to prove the conclusion: **"It is raining"**

---

**Notation:**

| Proposition                             | Symbol |
|------------------------------------|---------|
| It is raining                           | \( P \) |
| There is fog                        | \( Q \) |
| The boat race is held       | \( R \) |
| The rescue demonstration proceeds   | \( S \) |
| The prize is awarded              | \( T \) |

---

**Premises:**

1. If it is not raining or there is no fog, then the boat race will be held and the rescue demonstration will also proceed:

$$
(\neg p \lor \neg q) \rightarrow (r \land s)
$$

2. If the boat race is held, then the prize will be awarded:

$$
r \rightarrow t
$$

3. The prize was not awarded:

$$
\neg t
$$

---

**Proof:**

- From (2) and (3): using **Modus Tollens** *(negation)*:

$$
r \rightarrow t,\quad \neg t \Rightarrow \neg r
$$

- Substituting into (1):

  $$
  (\neg p \lor \neg q) \Rightarrow (r \land s)
  $$

  Since $$r \land s$$ is false (because $$\neg r$$), we get:

  $$
  \neg (\neg p \lor \neg q)
  $$

- Applying **De Morgan's Law**:

  $$
  \neg p \lor \neg q \Rightarrow r \land s
  $$  
  $$\Rightarrow \neg(\neg p \lor \neg q) \equiv p \land q$$

  ⇒ We get: $$p$$  
  ⇒ So **it is indeed raining**
